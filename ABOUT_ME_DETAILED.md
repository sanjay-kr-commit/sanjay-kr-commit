# About Me

Hi, I'm **Sanjay**, a Computer Science and Engineering student and developer interested in Linux, programming, developer tools, and the many small details that make computers interesting.

This page is meant to give a general idea of who I am, rather than trying to turn my profile into a list of achievements.

## Where it started

My interest in Linux began in **2020**, before I started college.

At the time, I was using a machine with **4 GB of RAM**, and Windows 10 was not working particularly well for the hardware. I started looking for alternatives and ended up trying Linux.

My first distribution was **Garuda Linux**.

It was an interesting way to enter the Linux world. From there I gradually moved to **Ubuntu**, then **Pop!_OS**, and eventually returned to **Arch Linux**.

The important part was not really the distributions themselves. Using Linux made me curious about what was happening underneath the desktop. I started learning about the operating system, command-line tools, configuration, processes, filesystems, and the different pieces that make a Unix-like environment work.

That curiosity stuck.

## From KDE to the shell

A lot of my early Linux experimentation happened in **KDE Plasma**.

I enjoyed configuring the desktop, changing things around, experimenting with different workflows, and learning how the environment worked. Over time, though, I became more interested in what was happening underneath the graphical interface.

That eventually led me toward the shell.

I started spending more time with Unix utilities, pipelines, environment variables, shell scripting, and eventually **zsh**.

The shell became one of my favorite places to work.

There is something appealing about being able to combine relatively small tools into something much larger. A command can become a script, a script can become a tool, and a collection of tools can eventually become a system.

That way of thinking has influenced how I approach programming in general.

## What I like working with

Most of my technical interests are around software and systems that I can understand, modify, and build on myself.

I particularly enjoy:

- Linux and Unix-like systems
- zsh and shell scripting

Sometimes that means going deeper into a problem than is strictly necessary. That's usually where the interesting parts are.

## Keyboards and the little things

I also really like **split keyboards**.

There is something satisfying about having a physical tool that can be configured and shaped around the way you work. And yes, **RGB lighting makes my world boom**.

It's a fairly small hobby compared with programming, but it fits the same general tendency I have toward customization and experimentation.

## Outside of computers

Computers are a large part of what I enjoy, but they're not the whole thing.

I like **anime, manga, books, novels, and music**. I sometimes sing, and I have also spent time learning music.

I tend to enjoy quieter and more atmospheric stories, especially ones that leave something to think about after finishing them.

I also like exploring subjects simply because they catch my attention. Not everything needs to become a project. Sometimes being curious about something is enough.

## How I approach things

I usually start with something practical or something that catches my curiosity.

Then I start asking questions.

How does it work? Why was it designed this way? Can it be simpler? Can I change it? What happens if I push it a little further?

Sometimes that ends with a small script.

Sometimes it becomes a larger project.

Sometimes I learn something and move on.

I like that process. I'm not trying to make every project into proof that I'm exceptionally talented. I'd rather understand what I'm doing, build useful things, and keep getting better.

I like having different things to explore.
